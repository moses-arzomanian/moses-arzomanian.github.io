Part 01
⏱ 20 min
How to Create a "Real" Team
A real team isn't just a group of people sharing a workspace. Define the features that distinguish genuine team performance — and establish who is responsible for each one.
"
A real team is a small number of people with complementary skills who are committed to a common purpose, performance goals, and approach for which they hold themselves mutually accountable.
Katzenbach & Smith — The Wisdom of Teams (1993)
F
Facilitator Note

Setup (5 min): Read the definition aloud, then ask participants to silently write what they think the key features of a real team are before discussing. This prevents groupthink.

Discussion (15 min): Go around the room. Capture features on a whiteboard as well. The "Responsible" column should spark healthy debate — push the group to be specific (a role, not just "everyone").

Feature of a Real TeamWho Is Responsible?
Psychological safety — members can speak up without fear
Team Leader
Clear roles with no critical gaps or overlaps
Team Leader + HR
Debrief Prompts
  1. Which features did the group agree on immediately? Which sparked debate?
  2. Where do gaps exist between our list and how we actually operate today?
  3. Which "responsible" assignments surprised you?
Part 02
⏱ 25 min
Identify Good and Bad Team Members
Honest reflection on what separates teammates who elevate a team from those who hold it back. This is about behaviors, not people.
F
Facilitator Note

Important framing (2 min): Before starting, emphasize that this exercise is about behaviors and patterns, not specific individuals. Ask participants to think of behaviors they have witnessed across teams, not people in this room.

Individual + Group (23 min): Give 7 minutes for silent individual writing, then open to group discussion. Aim for 8–10 traits per column. Watch for overlap between columns — traits like "poor communication" often have a counterpart worth naming explicitly on the left.

✓ Behaviors That Strengthen the Team✗ Behaviors That Weaken the Team
Gives credit openly and supports teammates' ideas
Takes credit for group wins, deflects blame onto others
Communicates proactively when something changes
Goes silent when behind or in conflict
Debrief Prompts
  1. Which behaviors on the right side are hardest to address directly? Why?
  2. Do any behaviors on the left side describe someone you've worked with and want to emulate?
  3. Which behaviors — positive or negative — are most present in this team right now?
Part 03
⏱ 90 min
Vision, Mission & Goals
A team without shared direction is just a group. This module anchors your team to the company's purpose, sets a shared vision, defines your mission, and turns both into concrete goals you can act on.
L&D Framework — Tuckman's Stages

This module moves teams from Forming (polite uncertainty) toward Norming (shared expectations). Cascading from the company's mission to your team's own mission and vision gives everyone a common reference point before conflict — the Storming stage — becomes productive rather than destructive.

Tuckman, B.W. (1965). Developmental sequence in small groups. Psychological Bulletin, 63(6), 384–399.

Part 3A — Vision Discussion & Statement
F
Facilitator Note

Discussion (25 min): Assign 2–3 questions per small group. Each group discusses for 10 min, then shares key themes with the room. Capture resonant phrases on a flipchart — they feed the Vision Statement below. All questions are forward-looking; push the group to think in terms of 1–3 years out.

Watch for: Answers that are too abstract ("we want to make a difference"). Push for specificity — "Make a difference to whom? By when? How would we know?"

Statement (15 min): Draft together on a whiteboard first, then refine. A strong vision statement is forward-looking, specific enough to guide decisions, and short enough to remember without reading it. Ask: "Would this sentence help us make a difficult decision?" If not, sharpen it.

01
What impact do we want to have — on colleagues, clients, or the people we serve?
02
What do we want to be remembered for as a team?
03
What should we never lose sight of, no matter how our goals evolve?
04
When we make decisions, what principles do we want guiding us most?
05
How do our daily actions bring us closer — or further — from our vision?
06
In what ways do we create value for people who never interact with us directly?
07
What would "thriving as a team" look like in concrete, observable terms?
08
How would we describe the "ideal version" of our team two years from now?
Objectives
  1. Align the team around a shared identity and direction.
  2. Practice distilling multiple perspectives into one unified statement.

Part 3B — Our Team's Mission
F
Facilitator Note

Format (20 min): Point the group to the company mission and vision above. Ask: "Given that context, what is our specific contribution?" Have pairs discuss 1–2 questions for 8 min, then share. Capture key phrases — they will feed the Mission Statement box below.

Watch for: Teams often confuse mission (why we exist / what we do now) with vision (what we aspire to become). If it starts with "We will become…" it's a vision, not a mission.

01
What is this team uniquely responsible for that no other team in the organisation is?
02
Who depends on our team, and exactly what do they depend on us to deliver?
03
If our team didn't exist tomorrow, what would break, stall, or go unnoticed in the organisation?

Part 3C — Goals Discussion
F
Facilitator Note

Format (30 min): Open with Q1–Q3 as a full group to align on the current state. Then break into pairs for Q4–Q8 (10 min each pair), focusing on questions most relevant to where this team is right now. Regroup for Q9 as a closing reflection.

Watch for: Conflicting answers on Q3 (how we measure progress) — this often reveals misaligned expectations. Surface it; don't smooth it over. It's productive tension.

01
What are our top priorities as a team right now?
02
Are our goals challenging enough to motivate us, but realistic enough to achieve?
03
How do we measure progress effectively?
04
Where do we lose focus or momentum as a group?
05
Which team achievements are you most proud of — and why?
06
What could make our goal-setting process clearer or more inclusive?
07
What would success look like for our team one year from now?
08
Where do we see gaps between our vision and our current reality?
09
Which of our recent actions best reflect our shared purpose — and which ones don't?

Part 05
⏱ 50 min
Rules & Culture
Every team runs on unwritten rules. This module makes them explicit — turning unspoken assumptions into shared agreements everyone can commit to and hold each other to.
L&D Framework — Lencioni's Five Dysfunctions

Rules and culture work addresses the first two levels of Patrick Lencioni's pyramid: Absence of Trust and Fear of Conflict. Teams that can't name their working norms explicitly tend to avoid difficult conversations — which cascades into lack of commitment, avoidance of accountability, and inattention to results.

Lencioni, P. (2002). The Five Dysfunctions of a Team. Jossey-Bass.
Part 5A — Rules Discussion
F
Facilitator Note

Format (25 min): These questions are personal. Give participants 8 minutes to write their answers individually before any group sharing. This protects introverts and surfaces more honest responses than open floor discussion alone.

Sensitive questions: Q5 (what destroys trust) and Q11 (mistakes without blame) can surface real tension. If the group goes quiet, that's data. Name it: "Silence usually means this one hits close to home. That's exactly why we're naming it."

01
When does our team feel most respected by one another?
02
What behaviors erode trust within our team?
03
How do we show respect to one another day to day?
04
What does "trust" look like in our team's everyday actions?
05
What small actions can quickly destroy trust in our team?
06
How does our team give and receive feedback effectively?
07
What makes teamwork feel smooth and energizing for us?
08
How do we handle it when teammates have different working styles?
09
What can we each do to make collaboration easier for the whole team?
10
What attitudes or behaviors make our culture stronger?
11
How can we address mistakes and failures without creating blame or fear?

Facilitator Note — Closing

The Charter (20 min): This is the most important 20 minutes of the day. Draw on everything the group produced — the vision (3A), the values (Part 4), and the rules (5B) — to briefly reflect on the journey before asking each person to sign. The act of signing is not a formality; it's a public commitment that changes how people hold themselves and each other accountable going forward.

What to do with it: Print this completed document and distribute to every team member within 48 hours. Post the charter somewhere visible — a shared channel, a meeting room, the team's intranet page. Revisit it at least quarterly.
